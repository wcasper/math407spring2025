**Problem 1:**

Prove that if $$G$$ is a group where every element has order $$2$$, then $$G$$ must be Abelian.
[Hint: consider the expression $$abab$$]

**Solution:**

Let $$a,b\in G$$.
Then

$$e = (ab)^2 = abab.$$

It follows that

$$ab = aeb = a(abab)b = a^2bab^2 = ebae = ba.$$

Since $$a,b\in G$$ were arbitrary, this proves $$G$$ is Abelian.

**Problem 2:**

Suppose that $$H$$ is a subgroup of a group $$G$$ and $$[G:H]=2$$.  Prove that $$H$$ must be a normal subgroup of $$G$$.
[Hint: if $$a\notin H$$, then the two left cosets of $$G$$ are $$H$$ and $$aH$$.  The two right cosets are $$H$$ and $$Ha$$.]

**Solution:**

We need to show that $$aH=Ha$$ for all $$a\in G$$.
If $$a\in H$$, then $$aH=H=Ha$$.
Otherwise, if $$a\notin H$$ then

$$ H\cup aH = G =  H\cup Ha.$$

Since $$aH\cap H=\varnothing$$ and $$Ha\cap H = \varnothing$$, it follows that $$aH = Ha$$.
This proves that $$H$$ is normal.

**Problem 3:** 

The **dihedral group** $$D_n$$ of symmetries of a regular $$n$$-gon consists of the subgroup of $$\text{GL}_2(\mathbb{R})$$ generated by the two matrices

$$
R = \left(\begin{array}{cc}
\cos(2\pi/n) & -\sin(2\pi/n)\\
\sin(2\pi/n) &  \cos(2\pi/n)
\end{array}\right),
\quad\text{and}\quad
S = \left(\begin{array}{cc}
1 & 0\\
0 & -1
\end{array}\right).$$

* a) Use induction to prove that

$$
R^k = \left(\begin{array}{cc}
\cos(2\pi k/n) & -\sin(2\pi k/n)\\
\sin(2\pi k/n) &  \cos(2\pi k/n)
\end{array}\right)$$

* b) Prove that $$SR^k = R^{-k}S$$

* c) Prove that

$$D_n = \{I,R^1,R^2,\dots, R^{n-1},S,RS,R^2S,\dots, R^{n-1}S\}$$

**Solution:**

* a) This follows from the angle addition formulas for sine and cosine.

* b) This is a simple calculation using the fact that cosine is even and sine is odd.

* c) We know that $$D_n$$ is the subgroup of $$\text{GL}_2(\mathbb R)$$ generated by $$R$$ and $$S$$.

Therefore $$D_n$$ contains all possible products of $$R$$ and $$S$$, so that

$$\{I,R^1,R^2,\dots, R^{n-1},S,RS,R^2S,\dots, R^{n-1}S\}\subseteq D_n.$$

Furthermore, one can check directly that 

$$\{I,R^1,R^2,\dots, R^{n-1},S,RS,R^2S,\dots, R^{n-1}S\}$$

is closed under multiplication and inversion, and is thus a subgroup of $$\text{GL}_2(\mathbb R)$$.
Since it contains $$R$$ and $$S$$, it must also contain the subgroup generated by $$R$$ and $$S$$.
Hence

$$D_n\subseteq \{I,R^1,R^2,\dots, R^{n-1},S,RS,R^2S,\dots, R^{n-1}S\}.$$

Putting things together, we get

$$D_n = \{I,R^1,R^2,\dots, R^{n-1},S,RS,R^2S,\dots, R^{n-1}S\}.$$


**Problem 4:**

Suppose that $$G$$ is a non-Abelian group with $$2p$$ elements (for $$p$$ an odd prime).

* a) Prove that $$G$$ must have an element $$h\in G$$ of order $$p$$.  (Hint: use Problem 1)
* b) Let $$H=\langle h\rangle$$ be the subgroup of $$G$$ generated by the element $$h$$ from part (a).  Prove that $$H$$ is normal.  (Hint: use Problem 2)
* c) Continuing with the notation of part (b), fix an element $$g\in G$$ with $$g\notin H$$.  Prove that $$g^p$$ has order $$2$$.  (Hint: consider the element $$gH$$ of the quotient group $$G/H$$.  Since $$p$$ is odd, $$(gH)^p\neq eH$$ and therefore $$g^pH\neq eH$$)
* d) One can show that the elements $$h$$ and $$g^p$$ behave like $$R$$ and $$S$$ from the dihedral group $$D_p$$, so that $$G$$ is isomorphic to $$D_p$$ (see Bonus Problem).  Use this fact to determine all groups of order $$14$$ up to isomorphism.

**Solution:**

* a) If all the elements of $$G$$ have order $$1$$ or $$2$$, then $$G$$ is Abelian by Problem 1.  Therefore $$G$$ must have an element of order $$h\in G$$ greater than $$2$$.
By Lagrange's theorem, $$h$$ has order $$p$$ or $$2p$$.
If $$h$$ has order $$2p$$, then $$G$$ is cyclic and thus Abelian.
Therefore $$h$$ must have order $$p$$.
* b) The subgroup $$H$$ has order $$p$$, so by Lagrange's theorem it has index $$2$$.  Thus $$H$$ is normal by Problem 2.
* c) Let $$g\in G\backslash H$$.  As a simple consequence of Lagrange's theorem $$g^{2p} = e$$.  Thus $$(g^p)^2 = e$$, so if we can prove that $$g^p\neq e$$ then $$g^p$$ must have order $$2$$.

We know that $$gH\neq H$$.  The subgroup $$G/H$$ has order $$2$$, so since $$gH$$ is not the identity in $$G/H$$, the order of $$gH$$ in $$G/H$$ must be $$2$$.  Since $$p$$ is odd, it follows that

$$gH = (gH)^p = g^pH.$$

In particular, this means that $$g^pH\neq H$$, so that $$g^p\notin H$$.   Hence $$g^p\neq e$$.

* d) If $$G$$ is a group of order $$14$$, then $$G$$ is either Abelian or non-Abelian.  If $$G$$ is Abelian, then $$G\cong Z_{14}$$.  If $$G$$ is non-Abelian, then we just found out that $$G\cong $$D_7$$.  Thus the only groups of order $$14$$ are $$D_7$$ and $$Z_{14}$$.


**Problem 5:**

* a) prime divisor form, and
* b) invariant factor form

**Solution:**

* a) Note that $$720 = 2^43^25$$.  Therefore the groups are

$$\mathbb Z_{2^4} \times \mathbb Z_{3^2}\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

$$\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_{2^3} \times \mathbb Z_{3^2}\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

$$\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_{2^2} \times \mathbb Z_{3^2}\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

$$\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_{3^2}\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

$$\mathbb Z_{2^4} \times \mathbb Z_3\times \mathbb Z_3\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

$$\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_{2^3} \times \mathbb Z_3\times \mathbb Z_3\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

$$\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_3\times \mathbb Z_3\times \mathbb Z_{5}$$

* b) Converting the groups from (a), we get

**Bonus Problem:**

Suppose that $$G$$ is a non-Abelian group with $$2p$$ elements (for $$p$$ an odd prime).
Let $$h\in G$$ have order $$p$$ and let $$H=\langle h\rangle$$ be the subgroup generated by $$H$$.
Fix an element $$g\in G$$ of order $$2$$.

* a) Prove that $$h^kg = gh^{-k}$$ for all $$k\in\mathbb{Z}$$.
* b) Prove that the function $$\psi: D_p\rightarrow G$$
defined by

$$\psi(R^k) = h^k,\quad\text{and}\quad \psi(R^kS) = h^kg$$

is a group isomorphism.
