### Directions
Solve the following problems and type up your solutions.  Your solutions should be provided in one of the following formats (in order of preference)
* typed up in $$\LaTeX$$ and submitted as a PDF on Canvas
* written legibly on blank paper, scanned into a PDF and then uploaded on Canvas
* written on ancient parchement with a quill and then flown to the instructor via owl post like in Harry Potter

If you go with the first strategy, you may wish to check out Overleaf which is a free and intuitive website for generating $$\LaTeX$$ documents online.
If you wish to use the second method and don't own a scanner at home, you can check out the numerous scanning apps available for smartphones.

**Problem 1:**

Show that a ring $$R$$ is a field if and only if the only ideals of $$R$$ are $$(0)$$ and $$R$$.

**Problem 2:**

Let $$R$$ be a commutative ring with identity, and suppose $$a_1,\dots, a_n\in R$$.
Prove that the set
$$I = \{x_1a_1+x_2a_2+\dots+x_na_n: x_1,\dots, x_n\in R\}$$
is an ideal of $$R$$.
This ideal is called the **ideal generated by** $$a_1,\dots, a_n$$ and usually denoted by $$(a_1,\dots, a_n)$$.

**Problem 3:**

Let $$R$$ be an integral domain.
Using the notation of the previous problem, let $$a_1,\dots, a_n\in R$$ and consider the ideal $$(a_1,\dots, a_n)$$.

* a) Show that if $$(a_1,\dots, a_n) = (b)$$, then $$b$$ is a greatest common divisor of $$a_1,\dots,a_n$$
* b) Using part (a), explain why every principal ideal domain (PID) is a GCD domain, ie. one where greatest common divisors always exist.

**Problem 4:**

Let $$F$$ be a field.  The ring $$F[x,y]$$ consist of all polynomials in two variables $$x$$ and $$y$$.
* a) Prove that $$F[x,y]$$ is not a PID
* b) Prove that $$F[x,y]$$ is a UFD


**Problem 5:**

Let $$F$$ be a field, and let $$I$$ be the principal ideal of $$F[x]$$ generated by $$x^2+x+1$$.
Consider the quotient ring $$R = F[x]/I$$.

* a) Show that if $$F = \mathbb Q$$, then $$R$$ is a field.
* b) Show that if $$F = \mathbb Z_2$$, then $$R$$ is a field.
* c) Show that if $$F = \mathbb C$$, then $$R$$ is not even an integral domain.
